1. As-Is Process

Current complaint handling flow in the retail bank appears to operate as a largely manual case-management workflow1. Customer submits complaint
   - Channels: branch, call center, email, web form, mobile app, regulator referral
   - data may be incomplete or unstructured

2. Manual triage
   - Operations staff read complaint
   - Identify complaint category, severity, product, customer
   - Assign to correct queue/team

3. Evidence collection
   - Staff gather transaction history, call recordings, account activity, KYC data, prior interactions, policy documents
   - Often requires accessing multiple systems manually

4. Policy and regulatory review
   - Reviewer checks complaint against internal policies, product terms, compliance obligations, and regional regulations

5. Draft response
   - Investigator prepares customer response
   - Response reviewed by supervisor/compliance where required

6. Closure
  /compliance where required

6. Closure
   - Case resolved, customer notified
   - Outcome logged for audit and reporting

2. Bottlenecks

Key operational bottlenecks and risks include:

- Manual triage
  - 40% routing errors create reassignment loops
  - High dependency on staff experience
  - Delays caused by queue balancing

- Fragmented evidence gathering
  - Investigators switch across systems
  - Missing data leads to repeated follow-ups
  - Long turnaround for call/audio retrieval

- Manual investigation
  - No standardized decision automation
  - Repetitive low-complexity complaints consume analyst time

- Policy interpretation variability
  - Different investigators may interpret rules differently
  - Inconsistent customer outcomes

- Response drafting
  - Manual writing and approvals slow resolution
  - High quality-control effort

- Operational scale issue
  - 50,000 complaints/month creates backlog spikes
  - Average 7–10 day resolution time increases regulatory and reputational risk

Proposed future-state flow:

4. STP Criteria

Complaints suitable for straight-through processing should meet predefined business and compliance rules such as:

- Complaint type is low complexity
  - Fee disputes
  - Duplicate charges
  - Minor service delays
  - ATM cash dispute below threshold

- Sufficient structured data available
  - Customer identity verified
  - Transaction references present
  - Required evidence accessible automatically

- Clear policy outcome exists
  - Rule-based determination possible
  - No subjective investigation required

- Low financial and regulatory risk
  - Compensation below threshold
  - No fraud indicators
  - No vulnerable customer escalation
  - No legal threat or regulator

- High confidence AI classification
  - Confidence score exceeds threshold
  - No conflicting indicators detected

- No prior unresolved linked complaint
  - Avoid duplicate or related-case conflicts

5. Retry, Timeout and Fallback

A resilient complaints workflow should include operational controls for automation failures.

Retry Logic
- Retry API calls for upstream banking systems
- Exponential backoff for temporary failures
- Retry document extraction/OCR when confidence is low

Timeout Handling
- Evidence retrieval timeout thresholds
- Escalate if dependency systems unavailable beyond SLA
- Auto-notify operations team for stalled cases
- Trigger manual intervention after defined wait period

Fallback Mechanisms- AI confidence below threshold → human review
- Missing customer data → exception queue
- Policy engine ambiguity → compliance escalation
- System integration failure → manual workflow continuation
- Response generation validation failure → supervisor workflow continuation
- Response generation validation failure → supervisor approval path

Audit and Monitoring
- Full logging of automated decisions
- Exception dashboards
- SLA tracking and breach alerts
- Human override capability
